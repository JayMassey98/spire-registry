[4000] SUPPORTIVE EVIDENCE
==========================

LAST UPDATED
------------
2026-10-15

PURPOSE
-------
This document is the top-level owner for the Supportive Evidence series,
defining its scope and organisation.

The Supportive Evidence series contains information that supports, validates,
justifies, or explains project conclusions, decisions, and activities.

SYSTEM GOVERNANCE
-----------------
The Supportive Evidence series is part of the wider knowledge-base system.

Ownership boundaries, hierarchy relationships, and evolution are defined in:

    [0000] REGISTRY OVERVIEW

CONTENT RULE
------------
Information in the Supportive Evidence series should explain why something is
believed, accepted, rejected, or retained.

The series includes:

- retained evidence
- validation results
- supporting analysis
- technical justification
- forensic findings
- retained hazards and failure modes

The series does not normally contain:

- established knowledge
- unresolved questions
- project objectives
- action lists

SERIES CONTENTS
---------------

[4100] RETAINED EVIDENCE

    Retained evidence, analysis, justification, and proofs.

SUPPORTIVE EVIDENCE RULE
------------------------
Evidence should be retained when it materially supports:

- conclusions
- decisions
- findings
- methodologies
- investigations
- historical understanding

Supportive evidence may include:

- successful validations
- failed validations
- confirmed hypotheses
- rejected hypotheses
- investigative dead ends
- contradictory observations
- forensic discoveries

Evidence does not need to prove a conclusion in order to be valuable.

Evidence that eliminates incorrect possibilities, records historical reasoning,
or prevents repeated investigation may be retained where future value exists.

The Supportive Evidence series should answer:

- Why is this believed?
- How was this validated?
- What alternatives were considered?
- What evidence exists?
- What lessons were learned?
